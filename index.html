Students' Introduction To Engineering (SITE)

Funded by the Vancouver School Board's Student Leadership Fund, Student's Introduction To Engineering (SITE) has been running for over three years, introducing almost 200 students to programming and electrical engineering. There are multiple workshops for different ages to inspire students to pursue engineering. These workshops have always been run by girls to encourage gender diversity and STEM and show female students that there are other girls interested in engineering.

SITE offers four different workshops, three for elementary school students: Micro:bit Explorers, Micro:bit Builders, and Circuit Engineers, and one for female high-school students: Introduction to Electronics. The three elementary school workshops are roughly two to three hours long each. Introduction to Engineering is four one-hour sessions, usually weekly. SITE is primarily for VSB students. Please read below for more details.

Micro:bit Explorers

Grades 4-5

Teaches students basic coding through a block-based language and teaches them how to use the Micro:bit, a simple microcontroller.

Micro:bit Explorers focuses on teaching the basics of computer programming, teaching the coding language Scratch, an easy-to-understand block-based language. Students will be taught how to code a name tag and an even-odd indicator, how to export their code onto the physical Micro:bit controller, and how to interact with the Micro:bit controller and run their code. This workshop requires access to a computer for each student (provided by the school), while all other materials are supplied. This workshop is roughly 2 hours long and the students don't need any previous programming experience.

Micro:bit Builders

Grades 5-6

Introduces students to electronics through a hands-on activity, coding a program for the Micro:bit, and building a circuit for it.

Micro:bit Builders teaches the basics of computer programming with a block-based interface for Scratch. Students will learn how to make a name tag, code an interactive guitar, build the guitar circuit, connect the Micro:bit to their guitar circuit, and interact with the guitar circuit and Micro:bit controller to play some musical chords. This workshop is roughly 2 hours long and does not require any previous experience in programming. This workshop includes a hands-on activity with all materials supplied by SITE, but still requires access to a laptop for each student provided by the school. The first project where students make nametags is the same project in Micro:bit Builders.

Circuit Engineers

Grades 6-7

Introduces electronic components and how to use them in a circuit with an Arduino microcontroller to move a Servo motor.

Circuit Engineers teaches basic electronic theory including Ohm’s law. Students will learn how to light an LED on a breadboard, and use various electrical components including photocell resistors and potentiometers to change the brightness of the LED. Students will also learn how to connect a servo motor (a small motor with an arm) to an Arduino microcontroller and a circuit that moves the arm of servo through changing the electrical current in the circuit. All materials will be supplied by SITE and this workshop does not use computers. This workshop is roughly 2 hours of hands-on activities and does not require any previous experience.

Introduction to Electronics

Highschool, female students

Teaches students electronic theory, the function of components, and how to program an Arduino to control various components.

Introduction to Electronics is a four session workshop on the basics of electrical engineering for female students to encourage gender diversity in STEM. Students will start by learning some theory including Ohm's law and parallel and series circuits before building some circuits of their own. Students will also learn how to connect a Servo motor to an Arduino microcontroller and program the Arduino to turn the Servo arm when a potentiometer is turned. This workshop includes many hands-on activities as well as a segment focused on programming. Any female or non-binary students are welcome to join. The students should use their own laptop or ones supplied by the school. All other materials will be supplied by SITE.

Compositions & Dances

Outside of STEM, my two main hobbies are dance and composition on the piano. I started dancing when I was two, picking up piano at 5, and I began my journey of composition when I was 13.

I named my first real composition How Did We Get Here? after the Minecraft advancement. Minecraft is the only PC game I've ever played and as I continued writing songs, I knew I wanted a theme to their names so I continued with the Minecraft advancement trend.

I'm less creative with dance, although I have choreographed a few ballet solos to my own compositions. I also do contemporary, and included my favourite solo, Last Flight, to The Dying Swan, at the bottom of this page.

How Did We Get Here?

How Did We Get Here? - Composition

This song will always be special to me. One summer afternoon, I just sat down on the piano bench, decided I wanted to write a waltz, and started playing the beginning melody of How Did We Get Here?. I was also working on the dance around this time and still hadn't found a title for the composition. I named the dance How Did We Get Here? first and realized the title fit both perfectly (and now I have a few songs named after Minecraft Advancements). There was no inspiration for this song, it simply happened, and I ask, with pride, "How Did We Get Here?"

How Did We Get Here? - Dance

When I listen to this song, I hear all the notes I wrote, but in some sections, I also hear dance steps. It wasn't hard to choreograph some parts of this solo because it sounded like there were a set of right steps that belonged to the music. After I finished the choreography with my teacher (Sibel Rivera) and saw how much we accomplished, I felt it had to be named How Did We Get Here?. This Minecraft advancement requires the player to have every status affect at once. This title is fitting because I am the composer, choreographer, and dancer, all at once.

Feels Like Home

Feels Like Home

High school was stressful, and after losing a family member, my mental health was not doing well. This was the first song I had written after How Did We Get Here?, after taking a two year hiatus from composing. Other than 16 counts of the melody, I wrote the whole song in four days. Similar to How Did We Get Here?, I sat at the piano and just started playing the melody, although this song was more influenced by my emotions. It's special because I had composed those other 16 measures before that family member passed away and it was one of the last phrases of music I played to them. Whenever I hear this song, it Feels Like Home.

Indelible

This dance came to me easier than How Did We Get Here? but the process was quite similar: I started in the middle and worked my way outwards. At first, I only had a few measures of steps for this dance, and I was worried that I wouldn’t finish the choreography in time, but then the rest of the dance, like my compositions, just came to be within the next two weeks. As it is my last year competing and I choreographed the entire dance on my own, it is truly unforgettable. The song Feels Like Home, but this dance is Indelible. I will be competing in early 2026 and share the video after I perform this solo.

Top of the World

Top of the World

This is my favourite piece that I have composed. I had the main melody stuck in my head for over a year, and for some reason, I just couldn't come up with the beginning. I was working on a different piece when a set of four measures just stood out to me matched the other melody perfectly. It's one of my harder pieces that will take a while to learn, so I tried other instruments in MuseScore (the software for playing the music) and now I have a wonderful orchestral version. Whenever I'm having a bad day, I just listen to this song, and I feel like I'm at the Top of the World.

The End

The End

The main melodies of The End come from a song I wrote over a year before How Did We Get Here?, and while I loved how pretty it sounded, I felt like it had too many ideas. I was going through the list of Minecraft advancements and saw The End and The End... Again... and realized I could split my original piece into two songs. It's a bit ironic both songs are named The End since the melodies were my first piece, but sometimes you just have to start at The End.

The End Again

I always loved watching the piano tutorials with the falling keys and I finally figured out how to replicate it! There's software that takes the MIDI file and displays the falling keys, which I can line up to MuseScore playing the keys from the same MIDI file. The engineer in me wants to set up an LED strip with an Arduino so I can have the visual with my hands playing as well, but I don't have a keyboard with a MIDI output, so I am leaving it with the MuseScore recordings for now. (And the fact that both songs are more difficult than the original so it will take time to learn them).

Hidden in the Depths

Hidden in the Depths

All the songs on this page are in a major key, and I seem to have trouble composing songs in a minor key (although I do have a few in progress). In fact, a friend challenged me to write a song in B minor, and while the harmony of the first measure follows a B minor chord progression, the key of the song is D major. The repetition of certain notes in this song makes me think of life slowly wearing away at something Hidden in the Depths. Instead of using a MIDI recording, I've decided to take the time to learn this piece since it isn't too hard to learn and the recording will have more of a soul. Coming "soon" :D

Last Flight

Last Flight

Even though I started ballet when I was 2, I only started contemporary when I was 13. Contemporary has some similarities to ballet, and my preferred style of contemporary is very close to ballet. I'm very lucky I had an amazing teacher (Naomi Wright) who chose Flight Not Yet Fled by Michael Wall, a cover of Camille Saint-Saëns's The Dying Swan, and choreographed this solo that perfectly suited my style of contemporary. While I tend to enjoy ballet more and have performed over 20 solos, this is my favourite by far.
